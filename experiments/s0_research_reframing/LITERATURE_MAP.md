# Literature map for context-conditioned target sound extraction

The established scientific home is **target sound extraction (TSE)** within informed/conditional source separation. Language-queried audio source separation (LASS), target speaker extraction, and audio-visual separation are conditioning-specific subfields. “Authentic extraction” describes an evaluation and deployment requirement; it is not yet a demonstrated new task or method.

Coverage includes primary papers and author implementations available through **10 September 2026**, emphasizing 2020–2026 and a few necessary earlier precedents. Dates below distinguish preprints from proceedings where verified. Results from unlike datasets are deliberately not ranked by their published SDR numbers. Repository availability means a public release was documented, not that its weights were downloaded or its results reproduced during S0. **NR** means not established in the inspected primary material, not proof of absence. Entries marked **screened** support only the stated limited conclusion; they are not deep implementation reviews.

The columns distinguish **G** (generative source distribution/latent synthesis) from **D** (deterministic regression/filtering). Both can reconstruct waveforms and both can make unsupported estimates. **MC** means an actual mixture-sum constraint, not merely conditioning on the mixture. No entry is treated as having proved authentic recovery of arbitrary weak real-world events.

Audio-visual event localization predicts **when** an event occurs; audio-visual segmentation predicts **which pixels** correspond to sound; localization estimates **where** a source is. None necessarily produces an isolated waveform. For example, [SelectTSL, July 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02343), explicitly targets selected-source direction/cardinality. These models may provide conditions or labels, but an improved localization score cannot substitute for extraction evaluation.

## 7. Ten works to read deeply, in priority order

1. **SAM Audio** — read architecture, data engine, faithful-separation statement, benchmark and human evaluation. It determines what the general-task claim cannot be.
2. **Personalized AEC** — establishes known playback plus target identity as prior art. Inspect exactly what is preserved during double talk.
3. **CLAPSep** — inspect training query construction, local mask/phase path and adapter placement; immediately actionable.
4. **SoundBeam meets M2D** — inspect where foundation features help and where signal detail must bypass semantic compression.
5. **TF-GridNet** — inspect complex mapping, phase behavior, compute and consistency loss; fallback when a bounded mask is limiting.
6. **Differentiable Consistency Constraints** — implement its algebra correctly and understand what it cannot guarantee.
7. **NKF-AEC** — understand adaptive filtering versus nonlinear modeling, synchronization and near-end preservation.
8. **OmniSep** — inspect composed positive/negative queries and prevent an unsupported multimodal novelty claim.
9. **MixIT**, with its sparse/semantic follow-up — inspect grouping degeneracy before using the existing recording as training data.
10. **SURF** — study the teacher-error assumptions and whether remixing improvements justify later generative adaptation.

USE, the 2011 dance-remastering paper and SoundBeam are mandatory prior-art checks even if they are not implementation priorities. FlowSep 2 and the September 2026 ontology paper are a current watchlist, not a reason to postpone data collection.

## 8. Does anything already solve essentially this problem?

**Yes, at the broad task/interface level:** prompted extraction from mixed sound, including multimodal cues, is already the aim of SAM Audio, OmniSep, SoundBeam and related work. Several systems support important parts of the requested experience. Known-music removal and remixing also have unusually close older precedents.

**Partially, at the required operating standard:** the inspected evidence does not establish extraction of this particular player's full impact/friction stream under real arcade interference while bounding unsupported additions, target omissions, timbre changes and abstention. A general model may already be adequate for some recordings; that must be measured rather than dismissed. Its failure on the hardest overlaps would not make its ordinary successful cases inauthentic.

The candidate research gap is **measured target fidelity under missing/contradictory evidence, with a temporally aligned known nuisance and independently acquired target evidence**. Whether this becomes a method contribution, a dataset/evaluation contribution, or primarily an application depends on S1–S4. Neither “multimodal separator,” “mixture-consistent,” “non-generative,” nor “uses a reference” is a defensible novelty claim by itself.

## 9. Source and release limitations

This is a structured research map, not an exhaustive systematic review or a reproducibility claim for every row. Primary full text/methods were inspected selectively for consequential design decisions; screened rows are explicitly marked. Current repository statements take precedence over a paper promising future release. In particular, SAM Audio access is gated, MMAudioSep weights are currently announced as forthcoming, OmniSep's README still lists release tasks, and historical SAM access failure is not a quality measurement.

The map's linked paper titles, dates and author repositories form the source inventory. Derived architecture choices and resource budgets elsewhere in S0 are proposals, not published benchmark results. Before paper submission, recheck releases and subsequent revisions, especially 2026 work, and extend the exact-reference/faithfulness search to any newly published results.
